## What Mortal Shell II gameplay looks like

Mortal Shell II is a soulslike action RPG built around **possessing the shells of dead warriors** and exploring an **interconnected open world**. You get a hub area — a Firelink-Shrine-style base where you gear up and level — then take an elevator or fast travel out into a large open map ripe for exploration.

Officially, Cold Symmetry frames it as "precision meets intensity in high-stakes combat" in a world blending the gothic and medieval with the otherworldly. In practice, per gameranx's hands-on impressions (551K views on *Before You Buy*), it can run "fast and frenetic, over the top, almost like a heavy metal album at times."

## The systems that define combat

### Shells — the main hook

Shells are the dead warriors you inhabit. On the surface they're skins, but each brings its own bonuses and abilities that can transform how you play: one turns **invisible** for backstabs, one has a **grappling hook**, one carries a **self-generating healing flask**, and one runs a risk-reward of **sacrificing half your health** for powerful hits. Each shell also carries its own small story you unlock by leveling it. If your health hits zero you're blasted out of your shell and fight on as your vulnerable base husk — win the fight and you can summon the shell back; die and you wake at the nearest shrine.

### Seals — rule modifiers

Seals are equippable items that change core mechanics: standard **blocks with timed parries and ripostes**, variants that **remove blocking entirely** for greater perfect-parry rewards, and the stand-out **harden** seal that lets you turn to stone mid-attack-animation to tank damage. One early seal, found in the hub area, makes the game easier overall.

### No stamina, generous parries

There's **no stamina meter** — swing and dodge freely. Parry timing is more forgiving than many genre entries. But this is still a harder-than-average game: some enemies can **one-shot you** (with an on-screen warning), and falls are a constant hazard the reviewer admits lost him "quite a bit of time."

### Weapons, tar stones and the tar forge

You carry light and heavy melee attacks plus a **ranged weapon** you can swap to anytime: a Doom-like shotgun to start, a crossbow, and even a **magic lute** that blasts enemies and afflicts them with confusion, making them fight each other. Back at the hub, the **tar forge** lets you spend consumables and currency to imbue weapons with perks; you also equip **tar stones** that add active and passive bonuses (poison build-up, better crits, more currency drops) and gain tiers the more you use them. A **resolve meter**, refilled by attacking and killing, fuels special attacks and temporarily empowers your weapons.

For how all eight melee weapons use these systems, see the [complete weapons guide](/guide/mortal-shell-ii-weapons).

## Structure and pacing

The game drops you into an open map with no quest markers: you buy vague hint maps from a vendor, then explore in any direction. The reviewer's favorite part was exactly that structure — "constantly stumbling upon new, different-looking biomes, strange new lands, hidden crypts, illusory walls, and weird-ass NPCs." Progression comes from hunting shells and weapons, which the systems make genuinely rewarding.

## Known rough edges

Honest impressions include real criticisms worth knowing before you buy: melee combos lack variation if you stick with one weapon too long, enemy variety is decent but many enemies feel similar to fight, a few areas feel barren between the interesting ones, and some stretches have no shrines nearby. None of it negates the verdict — "mostly a really good time" — but it's worth going in with eyes open. Full details in the [review roundup](/review/mortal-shell-ii-review).
