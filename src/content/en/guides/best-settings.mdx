Mortal Shell II's camera is the most common complaint in otherwise glowing player feedback — not because it is broken, but because a few default settings quietly fight sensitive stomachs. If the game makes you queasy, the cause is almost always one of three culprits: automatic camera correction, the camera mode, or sensitivity. Every one of them has a fix in the options menu, and the whole reconfiguration takes about two minutes.

## Camera mode: pick Action, not Calm

The game ships with two camera modes. **Calm** keeps the camera tighter over the shoulder, which feels cinematic for about ten minutes and then starts to press on your inner ear. **Action** pulls the view further back and opens up the field of view, which reduces the tunnel-vision effect that triggers discomfort for most people. If you are prone to motion sickness, start with Action — you can always switch later on feel.

## Turn off automatic camera alignment

This is the headline fix. Auto-alignment forcibly swings the camera back behind your character, yanking the view around without your input — the single most nausea-inducing element in the game. Switch it off and steer the camera yourself. Most players who report feeling sick report the problem evaporating after this single change.

## Lower the camera sensitivity

Sensitivity here is a single shared slider — there is no separate X-axis and Y-axis tuning. The catch: raising it makes vertical rotation feel disproportionately fast, which reads as jittery and unsettled. Set the slider moderately low and accept slightly slower horizontal turns; your vestibular system will thank you within the first fight.

## Controller prompts: match your pad

The controller icon option switches on-screen button prompts between Xbox layout (A/B/X/Y) and PlayStation 5 layout (triangle/circle/cross/square). Whichever pad is actually in your hands, set the prompts to match — fighting translated muscle memory mid-combat is its own kind of discomfort.

## Two bonuses outside the camera menu

- **Disable motion blur.** It is in the graphics settings rather than the camera menu, and disabling it removes a constant low-level smearing effect that sensitises your eyes during sprints and camera swings.
- **Hold the frame rate steady.** A locked, stable frame rate matters more for comfort than a higher average one. If your rig wavers, capping the framerate or dropping one quality tier usually steadies it.
